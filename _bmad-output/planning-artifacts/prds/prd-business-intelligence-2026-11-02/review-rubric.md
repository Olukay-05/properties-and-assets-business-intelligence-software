# PRD Quality Review — Property & Asset Intelligence

## Overall verdict

This is a strong, well-shaped PRD. Its thesis — "operational control + proof of work" — is explicit, the three workflows and platform foundations map cleanly onto it, and the success metrics (with counter-metrics) validate the thesis rather than measuring activity. Scope is honest (explicit Non-Goals, six indexed assumptions, six genuine open questions) and the FRs carry real testable consequences. The one material risk is done-ness on the compliance/NFR side: the NDPA commitments are declared pilot-readiness gates but are not traced to any FR with a testable consequence, so downstream story creation cannot extract "done" for breach notification, retention/deletion, or data-subject requests.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions, not "considerations." The NFR section cleanly separates commitments from hypotheses — §5.1 is a "hard requirement" while §5.2/§5.3 tag their thresholds `[ASSUMPTION: …]` ("target p95 API latency < 500ms … a sensible engineering objective, not a customer-validated requirement"). Trade-offs name what was given up, not just what was chosen: §7 "This product is **not** any of the following, in v1" and §8.2 enumerate deferred items with phase targets and reasons; the addendum's "Rejected alternatives (rationale preserved)" records why schema-per-tenant, a `geography` column, independent lat/lng columns, and a separate `approvals` table were rejected. The six Open Questions (§10) are genuinely open — a PostGIS-on-Neon spike (D1), MapLibre/OpenFreeMap licensing (D5), pricing, NDPC registration, NIPOST fees, National Cloud Policy — none is a rhetorical question with the answer already in the next sentence.

### Findings
- **low** No `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts at real tensions (§6.2 vs §10 Q6) — the PRD states "EU hosting is the approved deployment decision" while Q6 still requires confirming the "National Cloud Policy — … classification/residency position … before the pilot," and Q3 ("Pricing") is unresolved while SM-6 hinges on a signed paid plan. Both are surfaced (so nothing is hidden), but a `[NOTE FOR PM]` at each would mark the residual tension explicitly for the reader rather than leaving it to be inferred. *Fix:* add a `[NOTE FOR PM: EU hosting is approved but the National Cloud Policy position is unconfirmed — this is a pilot gate, not a settled matter]` callout near §6.2, and a similar one linking Q3 to SM-6.

## Substance over theater — strong

No furniture detected. Personas are three (Chidi, Amara, Tunde) and load-bearing — each UJ drives specific FRs via "Realizes UJ-*" tags, and FR-13/FR-21 explicitly span two journeys. Innovation theater is absent: the Vision itself disclaims novelty ("sold as **operational control + proof of work** — not as postcode lookup, AI, or advanced geospatial analytics"). NFR theater is absent: every non-functional item has a product-specific bound (LCP < 2.5s, p95 < 500ms, 99.5% monthly availability, WCAG 2.1 AA), not "scalable/secure/reliable" boilerplate. Vision theater is absent: the problem framing names the Nigerian FM context concretely ("spreadsheets, WhatsApp threads, and memory", "work orders are agreed verbally", "proof of work is weak, disputes are frequent, and billing leverage is lost") and would not swap into an unrelated PRD.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated and bets on a specific wedge: the product is sold as operational control + proof of work, and "ships only when three complete workflows work end-to-end and pass automated tests" (§1). Feature prioritization follows the thesis — the three workflows are exactly the arc of *know what's happening → get maintenance done → prove the work to the client* — and platform foundations (§4.4) are framed as underpinnings, not a grab-bag. Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than measure activity: SM-1 "≥80% of agreed pilot work orders are created and closed in the app (vs WhatsApp/paper)" and SM-3 evidence completeness test proof-of-work directly, not DAU/MAU. Counter-metrics are present and do real work — SM-C2 "do not inflate asset counts with junk entries to hit SM-4" guards against gaming SM-4. The MVP scope kind is coherently problem-solving (three complete workflows), and the scope logic in §8 matches that kind.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

The FR layer is genuinely strong — all 22 FRs carry at least one testable consequence, and several are precise and behavior-bound rather than aspirational (FR-19 "A request or job body that includes an `organisation_id` has it ignored"; FR-15 "Location on evidence is captured where available and does **not** block completion"; FR-11 "An approved Inspection cannot be edited; a correction creates a new version linked to the original"). The weakness is not in the FRs but in the compliance/NFR commitments, which are declared hard gates yet have no FR and no testable consequence to tell an engineer what "done" looks like.

### Findings
- **high** Compliance commitments are not traced to any FR (§6.1–§6.2) — the NDPA items are called "pilot-readiness items, not 'later' items," but breach notification ("promptly enough for the controller to meet NDPA's 72-hour clock"), the data-processing agreement, data-subject requests, and retention/offboarding deletion are not represented anywhere in FR-1…FR-22. An engineer building stories cannot source-extract acceptance criteria for the compliance workstream; the reader is pointed only to "the approved plan §11," which sits outside this document. *Fix:* add cross-cutting FRs (e.g., "FR-23 Record breach notification," "FR-24 Honour data-subject access/rectification/deletion") with testable consequences, or an explicit compliance Acceptance section mapping each §6.1/§6.2 commitment to a verifiable condition.
- **medium** Retention period is never given a value (§6.2) — "Retention: evidence and audit data retention is **defined and enforced**," and §6.1 says "document what is stored, why, and for how long," yet no duration appears in the PRD or addendum. A hard requirement asserting "defined and enforced" while leaving the number undefined is not verifiable. *Fix:* state a concrete retention period for evidence vs audit data (and for offboarding hand-back/deletion).
- **low** FR-2 required fields are not stated as testable consequences — the FR says a Property has "an address, site code, optional postcode, and location," but its consequences test only tenancy ("created under exactly one Client Account") and postcode non-blocking behavior. The address/site-code obligation is asserted but untestable as written. *Fix:* add "a Property records its address and site code, and the site code is unique within its Client Account."
- **low** Accessibility has a bound but no acceptance gate (§5.4) — "WCAG 2.1 AA" is a real bound (better than an adjective), but there is no FR, no test consequence, and no audit step to make "accessibility is built in, not postponed" verifiable. *Fix:* name the gate (e.g., an FR or acceptance line requiring a CI axe scan at AA plus a manual screen-reader/keyboard pass on the three workflows).

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit, not left for inference. §7 lists a broad Non-Goals set (client portal, public integration platform, desktop/offline/native, AI-drafted reports, preventive-maintenance engine, rent/lease/tenant, advanced geospatial, NIPOST 2+), and §8.2 de-scopes honestly by phase ("Deferred to Phase 3 (gated on evidence)", "Deferred to launch (Phase 2)", "Never"). FR-1 carries its own "Out of Scope" (invoicing, contracts, CRM, client logins). Six `[ASSUMPTION]` tags mark inferences the user did not directly confirm — including the personas themselves ("personas are realistic composites, not real people") — and all are indexed in §11. Open-items density (6 OQs + 6 assumptions) is proportionate for a pilot that is explicitly gated on evidence rather than a green-light-to-production launch, so it is not a blocker.

## Downstream usability — strong

Glossary (§3) defines 21 domain terms and instructs downstream readers to use them "verbatim." FR/UJ/SM IDs are contiguous and unique — FR-1…FR-22, UJ-1…UJ-4, SM-1…SM-7 plus SM-C1…SM-C3 — and every cross-reference I checked resolves: §5.1 is referenced by FR-19; "see UJ-3" in §2.3; SM cross-references (SM-1→FR-13/FR-15, SM-4→FR-2/FR-5/FR-6, etc.) all point at existing FRs. Sections stand alone on glossary terms rather than "see above." Each UJ has a named protagonist carrying context inline.

### Findings
- **low** "site" drifts as a synonym for "Property" (UJ-1 "needs their site live on the map"; SM-4 "≥85% of agreed assets have site + tag + category + condition + custodian") — the Glossary does define Property as "a client's site or premises," so it is acknowledged, but SM-4's "site" is meant to map to FR-2 (Property) and invites ambiguity with "Zone." The addendum also names the entity `site_zones` while the PRD term is "Zone." *Fix:* use "Property" in SM-4 and UJ-1, and align the addendum table name (`zones`) with the glossary term.

## Shape fit — strong

This is a multi-role B2B tool with real field UX (supervisors/technicians on ordinary Nigerian mobile networks), so UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing and correctly present rather than overhead. It is simultaneously a technical capability spec, and the PRD keeps that shape disciplined by pushing the "how" (stack, ADRs, entity set, endpoints, rejected alternatives) into `addendum.md`, leaving the PRD "capability-first" and about "what and why." As a chain-top document (feeds UX → architecture → stories), downstream traceability is appropriately emphasized, and it is neither over-formalized nor under-formalized for its stakes (a paid pilot).

## Mechanical notes

- **Glossary drift (minor):** "site" as a synonym for "Property" (see Downstream usability); addendum uses `site_zones` vs PRD "Zone" and `contractors` vs "Contractor." Capitalization of glossary terms ("Organisation," "Client Account") is otherwise consistent across FRs and UJs.
- **ID continuity (clean):** FR-1…FR-22, UJ-1…UJ-4, SM-1…SM-7 + SM-C1…SM-C3, and addendum ADRs D1…D10 are all contiguous and unique; no gaps, no duplicates, and all cross-references resolve.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip (clean):** six inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags (§2.3, §5.2 ×2, §5.3, §6.3, §9) all appear in §11; §11 has no orphan entries, and it correctly excludes hard requirements (RLS, accessibility, monitoring/audit, backups) from the index as commitments rather than assumptions.
- **UJ protagonist naming (clean):** all four UJs name a protagonist (Chidi, Amara, Tunde, Chidi) with context inline; no floating UJs.
- **Required sections (present):** Vision, Target User, Glossary, Features/FRs, Cross-Cutting NFRs, Constraints & Guardrails, Non-Goals, MVP Scope, Success Metrics, Open Questions, Assumptions Index — appropriate for a pilot B2B capability spec.
